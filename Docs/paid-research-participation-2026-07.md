# The structural finding that changes the whole plan

**No single platform provides steady income — the base layer is breadth, not depth.** This is verified from the platforms' own disclosures, not cynical Reddit takes: User Interviews itself says a typical participant gets invited to **1–2 studies per 6 months** ✓, and UK agencies enforce the MRS 6-month rule (Saros explicitly says "occasional only", ~2 studies/year) ✓. So "reliable monthly base" = registered on 10+ panels simultaneously, each firing occasionally. The only layer that can actually absorb your 10 hrs/week is AI-training work — everything else is opportunistic by design.

# Tier 2 — Specialist panels and expert networks (the lumpy high-£ layer)

**Respondent** is the verified anchor: $95 average incentive, most studies $50–$400+, focus groups $150–$250/session ✓, with third-party 2026 reviews corroborating ~$100+/hr real payouts and Respondent's own incentive guidance suggesting ~$200/hr for software developers ✓. Caveats: USD figures, 5% Tremendous payout fee, and screener acceptance limits volume. **User Interviews** — same tier, same low frequency; their own advice: complete profile, current job title, synced socials, honest specific screener answers, and they explicitly say AI-generated screener answers reduce approval ✓.

**Expert networks (GLG, AlphaSights, Guidepoint, Third Bridge, Dialectica)** — this didn't survive full verification, so treat as well-sourced but unconfirmed:

- SME owners and engineers are explicitly viable, not just C-suite — individual contributors typically $100–$250/hr, and smaller networks (e.g. DeepBench) list ordinary practitioners at $60–$300/hr.
- A first-hand Bogleheads account from an active consultant: works with 3 networks, averages 1–2 paid calls/week once established, payment pro-rated to the minute; he set his rate at triple the recommended figure and still got calls.
- Screener acceptance baseline ~20%; expert-network surveys pay $40–$80 each.
- AlphaSights, Third Bridge and Dialectica are London-headquartered — strong UK recruitment.
- Your genuinely rare angle: **marketplace-seller economics**. PE firms and hedge funds actively research eBay, Vinted, Etsy-class marketplaces and the collectibles/LEGO-investing niche. A seller doing £80k/yr across four platforms with a decade of fee/algorithm/policy history is exactly the "current operator" they struggle to find. Write your network profiles around that, not "software developer".
- Risks: compliance is strict (share non-public info once → permanently blacklisted), GLG negotiates rates down aggressively, and some proposed engagements border on competitive-intel fishing — decline anything probing your own suppliers/contracts.
- Scams: GLG's own disclosures confirm active impersonation of their brand ✓ — fake recruiters on free email domains asking for bank details before any call is scheduled. Verify domains, never give payment details pre-engagement.

# Tier 3 — AI-training contractor work (the hours sink, with risk priced in)

This is the only layer that scales to 10 hrs/week, and the picture in mid-2026 is materially worse than the 2025 blog posts suggest. Ranked for a senior UK coder:

1. **Mercor** — probably your best first application. Trustpilot 4.4 (533 reviews); advertises ~$90/hr for research tasks; positive reviewers consistently report weekly on-time payment. But: task droughts, and a UK contractor was terminated without explanation after 145 hours on a still-running project. Zero job security is the norm.
2. **DataAnnotation** — Trustpilot 4.0; didn't get deep verification this round but has the fewest severe complaints in the comparison set.
3. **Outlier (Scale AI)** — highest volume, most risk. Claims $500M+ paid, weekly Tuesday payouts ✓ — but carries an F BBB rating, an active lawsuit (Rogowicz v. Smart Ecosystem, alleging misclassification and wage theft), and documented rate compression: projects at $28–35/hr in early 2025 restructured to $18–22/hr by Q1 2026. Engineers holding >95% quality scores report $22–35/hr; deactivation happens without explanation or appeal, and "Oracle" status doesn't protect you. (Note: one claim — that Outlier publishes no concrete rates — was *refuted* 0–3, meaning rates do appear on their pages, but no specific UK £/hr figure survived verification.)
4. **Alignerr** — headline Trustpilot 4.6 is misleading: the reviews mostly rate the AI *interview* experience, not paid work. Documented speculative hiring (onboarding before work exists), and a "per finished hour" model that turned advertised $45/hr into ~$6.40/hr effective for one voice project. Low priority.
5. **micro1** — Trustpilot 4.7, worth an application, thin evidence either way.

Industry-wide sanity band (single source): legit platforms pay $30–$130/hr, mid-tier coding evaluation ~$75/hr, senior up to ~$110/hr on Mercor.

**Scam patterns specific to this space** (the black-market finding was verified ✓): a documented market sells verified EU Outlier accounts for $70–$100 plus $30–50 "fraud extensions" — which is *why* these platforms run aggressive ID verification and hair-trigger deactivations. Never buy/share accounts. Also: lookalike domains (outlierai.com vs the real outlier.ai), recruitment conducted over Telegram/WhatsApp, any upfront fee, and implausible pay for low-skill work are all hard walk-aways.

# UX testing — deprioritise it

The 2026 reality is poor for your profile. UserTesting post-Thoma Bravo: still pays reliably ($10/20-min test, $30–120 live interviews) but typical earnings have fallen to $20–100/month, screener acceptance is down to ~5–8%, and contributors burn 15–30 unpaid screeners per accepted test — that's a terrible use of hours you could sell at $75/hr. Userlytics: $5–90/session but weeks-long droughts, unpaid mid-test screen-outs, PayPal on ~15-day cycles. Keep accounts on both, only take moderated/live sessions ($30+), skip the $10 grind entirely.

# Tax — one verified trap that would actually cost you money

- Research incentives beyond receipted expenses **are taxable income**, but per HMRC EIM71105 (cited by the HRA guidance) they're unlikely to count as "earnings" for PAYE/NI — nothing deducted at source, you self-report via Self Assessment ✓. With the personal allowance already consumed by Hadley Bricks, they're taxable from the first pound.
- **Do not claim the £1,000 trading allowance against this income.** Verified against ITTOIA 2005 Part 6A, HMRC BIM86018 and LITRG ✓: it's a *single* allowance across ALL your trades, and electing it replaces *all* actual expense deductions in every trade. With Hadley Bricks expenses far above £1,000, claiming it would increase your tax. Just report the side income as additional self-employment/miscellaneous income and keep full expense deductions on the business. (If you ever incorporate, the company's income sits outside this calculation entirely.)
- Expert-network and AI-platform income is straightforward trading income, not a "research incentive" — invoice it/report it like any freelance work. NI nuance: the statutory exemption discussion is Class 4; Class 2 has been voluntary since April 2024.

# Screener strategy (verified against recruiter guidance)

Recruiters pre-filter from panel profile data before sending screeners — a complete profile directly increases invitations. They run consistency checks across answers and against stored profile data; conflicts get auto-flagged. The main reason honest panellists get screened out is *vague* free-text answers, not wrong demographics — ~30 words of specific, believable detail ("I list 40–60 items/month on eBay and Vinted, use Bricqer for inventory") beats both vague answers and implausible "power user of everything, daily" claims, which get rejected as exaggerated. Never fabricate — flagging is permanent per platform.
