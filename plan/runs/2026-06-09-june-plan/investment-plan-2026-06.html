1 · Executive summary

A guaranteed 2% real return to 2035 is not purchasable. The risk-free real rate (index-linked gilts held to maturity) is ~1.6% at the 2035 maturity. From a US Shiller CAPE of ~40, history puts the odds of equities beating 2.1%/yr real over 9 years at roughly 1-in-4. The current ~87%-risk-asset portfolio therefore makes the retirement date a market bet.

But the actual goal — £60k/yr of spending from June 2035 — can be substantially locked, because index-linked gilt real yields (1.6–2.3% at 2035–2045 maturities, June 2026) currently sit at or above the plan's required return at exactly the right maturities. The plan:

  1. FLOOR: buy an index-linked gilt ladder paying £60k (today's money) in every year 2035–2045. Cost: ~£498k (31% of investable assets). Extend to 2051 (~£216k more) by ~2029. Held to maturity: immune to markets, inflation-linked, backed by HM Government.
  2. GROWTH: ~£1.0M stays in global equities. Its job shrinks to funding the years after 2046 net of two state pensions — a bar it clears in 100% of historical high-valuation starts (and 99.6% of all 1,502 twenty-year windows since 1881).
  3. RATCHET: every June, bank equity strength into more locked years. One-way; never re-risk.
  4. Free wins: state-pension repair via part-time HB (Chris) and a decision needed for Abby; tax-free withdrawal sequencing worth ~£0 tax until the 2050s; crypto exit at 18% not 24%; deferred Accenture pot transfer.
The trade you accepted: in median markets the plan reaches ~£2.7M by 2035 instead of ~£3.0M; at the 90th percentile ~£4.2M instead of ~£5.1M. In exchange, the bottom quarter of outcomes — where the current portfolio delivers £37k/yr instead of £60k — is eliminated for 2035–2045 outright, and for 2046+ in every high-valuation start in 155 years of data.

4.3 Build sequence

  1. Tranche 1 (weeks 2–6): sell LS80 in II ISA → buy 2035–2037 rungs (~£150k). Shortest rungs first — they're the least replaceable.
  2. Tranches 2–4 (by ~Q1 2027): complete 2038–2040 (ISA) and 2041–2045 (SIPP). Phasing over 6–9 months averages the yield entry; don't stretch longer — every month at 87% risk from CAPE 40 is the risk you've chosen not to carry.
  3. Q3–Q4 2026: transfer Chris ACN £190k → II SIPP (after L&G written confirmations). Consolidate Vanguard ISA LS80→LS100 (stays as growth; second platform = deliberate resilience). Check Abby's L&G menu for an ILG index fund (~50% switch if available — its long duration roughly matches her 2043+ access).
  4. 2026–2029: extension rungs 2046–2051 from new savings (£11.5k/yr ISA), crypto proceeds, and ratchet sales.

4.4 The ratchet (mechanical, written down)

Each June: if growth sleeve > 1.25 × PV(unfunded future spending net of state pensions and existing rungs) → sell growth, fund the next rung(s). Otherwise do nothing. Never sell ladder to buy equities. Suspend only if the next unfunded year is ≥12 years away and ILG real yields < 0.5%.

4.5 Crypto exit (~£54k)

  1. Establish base costs (gain size drives the schedule).
  2. Interspousal transfer to Chris — CGT-free; he's basic-rate (18% vs Abby's 24%), with ~£24k of band headroom.
  3. Use both £3k annual exemptions; split across 2026/27 + 2027/28 if gains are large.
  4. Proceeds → ISA allowances (£20k each) → ladder rungs.

Lifetime income-tax cost of funding £60k/yr on this sequencing is roughly 2–5p in the pound from the mid-2050s and ~zero before — because the bridge is ISA-funded by construction and both personal allowances are deliberately filled from pensions every year from 2040. This is the quiet second benefit of holding the bridge rungs in ISAs.

5.2 Sequencing rules

  • Spend taxable-last: ISA rungs (forced, 2035–40) → pension-within-allowance (2040+) → TFC chunks → taxable pension → ISA capital last for flexibility.
  • Crystallise gradually (UFPLS or phased drawdown), not all at once — preserves TFC optionality and stays under tax bands. Note the recurring Budget rumours about capping tax-free cash: a reason not to leave crystallisation too late into the 2040s (watch item, not actionable now).
  • MPAA: once Chris flexibly accesses taxable pension income (2040+), his pension contributions cap at £10k/yr — irrelevant under this plan (no contributions planned post-2035).
  • Ratchet continues in retirement: each rung spent is one year off the runway; each good equity year adds one at the far end. Target ≥10-year funded runway at all times. After a bad equity year, don't extend — the runway IS the sequence-risk insurance.

5.3 Inheritance tax (announced change — affects this plan)

From April 2027 (announced Oct 2024 Budget; legislation in progress), unused pension funds fall into the estate for IHT. With house + portfolio, your estate will exceed the combined NRBs. Implications: (1) the old logic of "preserve pensions, spend ISAs, pensions pass IHT-free" is dead — this plan's pension-first drawdown from 2040 is already the right shape; (2) spouse-to-spouse remains exempt — the exposure is on second death; (3) gifting capacity opens up post-2040 once the floor is secure. Not modelled further here; revisit ~2030.

5.4 The annuity option (evaluate at Abby's 57th, Aug 2043)

At June 2026 rates a joint-life RPI-linked annuity at ~60 pays ~4.3–4.5% per £100k (estimated from published single-life RPI and joint-level ratios — get real quotes). If rates remain near this in 2043–48, annuitising ~£700–800k of pension would lock the entire post-2054 residual (~£35k/yr) plus all longevity risk, making the plan market-proof for life. It is an option, not a dependency — the ladder + state pensions may already suffice. Trigger to revisit: joint RPI-linked rate ≥ ~4.5% while the growth sleeve is above its ratchet threshold.

6.3 Modelled upside (what you forgo)

Median all-history outcome: proposed £2.71M vs current £2.99M at 2035 (−9%); boom case £4.18M vs £5.14M (−19%). In every scenario above ~p30 you'd have been richer staying put. That is the insurance premium, and it is paid only in the states of the world where you didn't need the insurance. Upside that survives the plan: ratchet converts strong markets into a floor reaching 2051+ years early, growth sleeve compounding into your 70s, HB profits, triple-lock drift, and the house.

6.4 Residual failure modes (what the data cannot rule out)

  • UK sovereign default on index-linked debt — no modern precedent; would coincide with collapse of every alternative plan too.
  • Japan 1990–2010 analogue on a global portfolio — equities at <−0.7%/yr real for 20+ years has never occurred in US data (worst −0.2%); it did occur in single-country Japan. The sleeve's ~52% US / 23% UK / 25% other spread is the mitigation; it is not a guarantee.
  • CPIH materially understating your personal inflation — rung payouts drift below real needs; ratchet margin and spending flex absorb modest drift.
  • Legislation — NMPA to 58, SPA acceleration, wrapper/tax changes (§7 R3).
