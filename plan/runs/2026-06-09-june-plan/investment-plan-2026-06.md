## 1. Executive summary

**The honest headline first: a guaranteed 2.0% real return to 2035 is not purchasable today.** The risk-free real rate (index-linked gilts held to maturity) is ~1.6% at the 2035 maturity. Anything above that requires risk, and from a Shiller CAPE of ~40, history says a 9-year equity bet beats 2.1% real only ~1 time in 4. Chasing the 2% number with your current ~85%-equity portfolio is close to a coin flip with a catastrophic tail.

**But 2% real was never the actual goal — it was a proxy for "retirement funded".** That goal *can* be locked, because the index-linked gilt curve currently pays 1.6–2.3% real at exactly the maturities matching your spending years:

1. **Buy the retirement directly.** An index-linked gilt ladder paying £60k (real) in each year 2035–2045 costs **~£498k today** — 31% of your investable assets. Held to maturity, it delivers regardless of what markets do. Extend it to 2051 (Chris's state pension) over the next ~3 years for ~£216k more.
2. **Everything else stays in growth** (~£1.0M global equities). After the floor extension, this sleeve only needs to cover spending **from the 2050s onward, net of two state pensions** — which it achieves even with ~0% real return over 20+ years. The worst 20-year real return for diversified equities in 155 years of data is −0.2%/yr (and +0.5%/yr from high-valuation starts).
3. **Claim the free wins:** state pension top-ups (each voluntary year costs ~£957 and buys ~£358/yr index-linked for life — ~270% "yield"), exit the £54k crypto position tax-efficiently, transfer the deferred Accenture pot for instrument access, and close the £69.5k → £60k spending gap, which remains the single largest risk in the plan.

**What you give up:** if equities compound at 7% real from here, the floored portfolio reaches ~£2.6M by 2035 instead of ~£3.1M. That is the premium paid for converting "probably fine" into "funded by contract with HM Government". You stated you accept this trade.

## 4. The strategy: floor + growth (liability matching)

Split the portfolio by *job*, not by percentage:

- **FLOOR — buys the years 2035–2051.** UK index-linked gilts, one maturity per spending year, held to maturity inside ISA/SIPP. Each rung pays out £60k of real purchasing power in its year. No market risk if held; credit risk = HM Government; inflation risk = hedged by construction (RPI/CPIH-linked).
- **GROWTH — buys the years beyond, and the upside.** Global equities. Because the floor covers 2035–2051 and state pensions cover £25.1k from 2054, the growth sleeve's *required* job collapses to ~£35k/yr (real) from the mid-2050s — a bar so low that no 20-year period in US history would have missed it, including starts from today's valuations.
- **RATCHET — one-way de-risking.** Each year, if growth assets exceed 125% of the remaining unfunded liability, sell some and extend the ladder another year. Never the reverse (no re-risking). Equity strength gets banked into certainty; equity weakness leaves a 10–15 year runway before it matters.

### Why this beats "85% equities and hope" — quantified

Tested against 155 years of monthly data (1,629 rolling 9-year windows; the app's ERN dataset):

| | Current portfolio (~85% eq) | Floor + growth |
|---|---|---|
| P(portfolio ≥ 2% real to 2035), from CAPE≥30 starts | ~25% | ~25–35% (slightly better; floor yields 1.6–2.3%) |
| Repeat of worst 9-yr window (Oct 1999 start, −6.3%/yr) | Portfolio → ~£1.25M; supports ~£38k/yr — **plan fails** | Floor still pays **£60k/yr 2035–2045 in full**; growth sleeve recovers on a 20-yr clock |
| Repeat of worst 20-yr window from high CAPE (+0.5%/yr) | Severe sequence risk through the bridge years | Fully funded with margin |
| Failure requires | A bad-but-ordinary equity decade (1-in-3 odds from here) | UK sovereign default on ILGs, or >20 yrs of < −0.7%/yr real global equity returns (never recorded in US data; Japan single-market analogue) |

The point is not that the floor raises the odds of hitting "2.0% portfolio return" — it barely does, and no honest plan can. The point is that it **decouples your retirement from the question**. With the floor in place, the difference between good and bad markets is the size of your surplus, not whether you eat.

### 5.3 Build sequence and timing risk

Do **not** execute the entire £500k equity→gilt switch in one trade. Phase it over ~6–9 months (e.g. £55–85k/month), for two reasons: real yields move (averaging your entry), and ILG phone-dealing in size is easier in tranches. But do not stretch it beyond that — every month at 85% equity from CAPE 40 is the risk you've decided not to carry. Buy the *shortest* rungs first (2035–2038): they're the least replaceable years.

### 5.4 The ratchet rule (write it down, follow it mechanically)

> Each June, compute: growth sleeve ÷ PV(unfunded future spending net of state pensions and existing rungs). If > 1.25, sell growth to fund the next unfunded rung(s). If ≤ 1.25, do nothing. Never sell the ladder to buy equities. Suspend ratchet purchases only if the next unfunded year is ≥ 12 years away and ILG real yields < 0.5%.
