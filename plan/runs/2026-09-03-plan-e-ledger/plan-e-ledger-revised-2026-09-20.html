2. The investment types

Index-linked gilt ladder, £909k

One UK index-linked gilt maturing each year from 2035 to 2045. Each rung is bought now, held to maturity, and pays out its inflation-adjusted value in the year it matures. The ladder balances in section 3 are held-to-maturity values accruing at the locked real yield, not market prices, which is why they rise steadily to 2035. Rungs for 2035–40 must be ISA money because that is the only pot reachable before Chris's pension opens in Nov 2040. Rungs for 2041–45 sit in the SIPP and mature inside it. Costs below are at the 17–20 Sep 2026 curve and are what each gilt actually costs on II for that redemption: the price includes the coupon stream, so the earlier "£108k a rung for £909k" (a zero-coupon figure) was never buyable and the coupons were counted twice in the words. Each rung now redeems £98.7k real in the ISAs and £106.3k in the SIPP, and the coupons, about £6.8k a year while the ladder is whole falling to under £1k by 2044, are paid separately into the wrapper that holds the gilt and are the top-up, not part of the rung. Same money as before; a different shape, with more arriving early.

Equity sleeve, £652k

Global equity trackers only: VS100 or LS100 at II and Vanguard, the L&G Global Equity Tracker in both Accenture pensions. It is not touched until 2046 in the planning case. From 2035 the ladder pays more than the household spends. Because each rung matures inside its wrapper, the surplus never leaves the ISA in 2035–40 and never leaves the SIPP in 2041–45, so it is reinvested in equity there with no new allowance needed. The sleeve rebuilds itself from guaranteed money rather than from market returns. Abby's salary sacrifice adds roughly £33k rising to £46k a year to her DC pot until 2035 (Aug 2026 payslip basis).

Cash and crypto, £120k

About £66k of savings is the buffer for lean Hadley Bricks years, cars and roof-type costs before 2035. The standing rule is that shortfalls come from here, never from trimming Abby's salary sacrifice. The crypto is held as a separate bet. Exiting it across two tax years was the June intention and is still a decision to make on its own.

Reading 2035–40: each ISA rung redeems about £99k inside the ISA and the ISA gilts still outstanding pay about £4.8k of coupons on top, £47k leaves it to cover spending after Hadley Bricks, and the rest (about £56k in 2035, less later as the coupons fall away) stays in the ISA as equity, which is why ISA equity climbs and GIA stays empty. Before 2035 the coupons (about £6.8k a year across both wrappers) are reinvested in equity where they land. Reading 2041–45: the SIPP rungs mature inside the pension, so they show as pension equity growing rather than as cash arriving. Spending in those years is covered by a £12.6k draw inside Chris's personal allowance plus tax-free cash. Years 2047 to 2049 are omitted as they are near-identical to 2046, and after 2050 the table shows every fifth year.

What the flat-2% tail hides. Because the drawdown rule takes only what fits inside the personal allowances, the pensions are never drawn down and reach about £1.88M by 2075. That is efficient for income tax, with about £500 paid across forty years, but it leaves a large estate for inheritance tax and beneficiary tax. From the mid-2050s the sensible move is to draw the pensions up to the basic-rate band and gift or ISA the excess. That decision is parked, not forgotten.

5. Assumptions

Money
All figures in Sep 2026 pounds. Balances from the tracker on 1 Sep 2026.
Gilt ladder
Priced at the 17–20 Sep 2026 curve (portfolio real IRR 2.01% ISA rungs, 2.29% SIPP rungs), coupon-inclusive, sized by the money committed: £519.5k of ISA money buys £98.7k of redemption a year 2035–40, £389.9k of SIPP money buys £106.3k a year 2041–45. Coupons paid out annually and reinvested in-wrapper; rungs held to maturity. Re-price on the day; each rung moves a few £k per 0.25% of yield. Order sheets: node scripts/gilt-ladder.mjs --budget 519500 --years 2035-2040 and --budget 389900 --years 2041-2045.
Equities
2% real a year in the planning case. Global tracker exposure throughout. Crypto is modelled as equity.
Cash
0.5% real. Buffer held at a £30k floor before any taxed pension draw.
Saving to 2035
Only Abby's salary sacrifice is credited, £33.5k rising to £46.2k a year into her DC pot (Aug 2026 payslip basis; was £28.9k–£40.9k on the April payslip). The rest of the household is treated as breakeven, which is the Amendment 1 cash-flow view with Hadley Bricks at £25k. Anything saved beyond that is margin.
Spending
£60k real from June 2035, flat for life. About £51k of it is the base and the rest is holidays.
Hadley Bricks
£13k a year from retirement to Nov 2040, tax-free inside Chris's personal allowance, then nil.
Pension access
Chris Nov 2040 at 57, Abby Aug 2043 at 57. Drawdown is phased: taxable draws to fill each personal allowance, then tax-free cash, then ISA, then GIA, then cash, then taxed pension at 20% as a last resort.
State pension
£12.5k each at 68: Chris from 2052, Abby from 2055. Both assumed to reach full records.
Tax
Personal allowance £12,570 and basic-rate limit £50,270 held flat in real terms, which is conservative. Tax-free cash capped at £268,275 each. That cap is frozen in cash terms, so the model is generous on it by roughly a third by the 2050s.
Surplus
In 2035–40 the surplus stays inside the ISA where the rung matured. In 2041–45 it stays inside the SIPP. After that anything left goes into ISAs up to £40k a year, then a general account, where dividend and gains drag is not modelled.
Not counted
The house, any inheritance, Hadley Bricks after 2040, Abby's bonus, Child Benefit after 2035, and any rule changes to pension age, tax-free cash or state pension age.
